# FDA Approval Summary: Capivasertib with Fulvestrant for HR-positive, HER2-negative Locally Advanced or Metastatic Breast Cancer with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN Alterations

- **PMID**: 39159418
- **PMCID**: PMC11588547
- **DOI**: 10.1200/jco.24.00427
- **Journal**: Journal of clinical oncology : official journal of the American Society of Clinical Oncology
- **Year**: 2024
- **Authors**: Dilawari Asma, Buturla James, Osgood Christy, Gao Xin, Chen Wei, Ricks Tiffany K., Schaefer Timothy, Avasarala Sreedevi, Turcu Francisca Reyes, Pathak Anand, Kalavar Shyam, Bhatnagar Vishal, Collazo Justin, Rahman Nam Atiqur, Mixter Bronwyn, Tang Shenghui, Pazdur Richard, Kluetz Paul, Amiri-Kordestani Laleh

## Abstract

#### Purpose:

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved capivasertib in
combination with fulvestrant for adult patients with hormone receptor
(HR)–positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast
cancer (MBC) who have received at least one prior endocrine therapy and
whose tumors harbor one or more *PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN*
alterations, as detected by an FDA-approved test.

#### Patients and Methods:

Approval was based on CAPItello-291, a randomized, double-blind,
multicenter trial of 708 patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced
or MBC, including 289 patients with *PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN* tumor
alterations. Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive capivasertib 400mg
twice daily for four days per week with fulvestrant vs. placebo with
fulvestrant. Randomization was stratified by presence of liver metastases,
prior treatment with CDK4/6 inhibitors, and geographical region.

#### Results:

A statistically significant PFS benefit was demonstrated in the
overall population (hazard ratio 0.6; 95% CI: 0.51, 0.71); this result was
driven by 289 patients in the biomarker-positive population (hazard ratio
0.5; 95% CI: 0.37, 0.68). An exploratory analysis of investigator assessed
PFS in the 313 (44%) patients in the biomarker-negative population showed
uncertain benefit (hazard ratio of 0.78; 95% CI: 0.60, 1.01). With
capivasertib, more patients had Grade ≥3 toxicities . Key concerns
included hyperglycemia (18% all-grade, 2.8% Grade ≥3), cutaneous
toxicity (58% all-grade, 17% Grade ≥3), and diarrhea (72% all-grade,
9% Grade ≥3).

#### Conclusion:

Capivasertib with fulvestrant was approved for patients whose tumors
harbored *PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN* alterations. Benefit-risk
assessment in this subgroup was favorable based on a statistically
significant and clinically meaningful improvement in PFS in the context of
an acceptable safety profile including no evidence of a potential detriment
in OS. In contrast, the benefit-risk was unfavorable in the
biomarker-negative population.

## Full text

#### INTRODUCTION

Recent drug development in metastatic HR-positive, HER2-negative breast
cancer has focused on targeted pathways altered in this disease. Molecular targets
include the *PI3K/mTOR* pathway, activating mutations in the
ligand-binding domain of the *ESR1* gene, and HER2-low
expression^[1]–[4]. Some trials supporting FDA approvals were
designed to evaluate agents’ safety and efficacy in a biomarker-positive
population only; however, other trials enrolled both biomarker-positive and
biomarker-negative populations. Trials enrolling both biomarker-positive and
biomarker-negative populations are often powered to look only at the overall
population and the biomarker-positive population to demonstrate efficacy. However,
even when trials are not powered to look at the biomarker-negative population, the
benefit-risk assessment in that population must also be favorable.

The *PI3K-AKT-mTOR* pathway is one of the commonly activated
pathways across cancer types and is involved in the regulation of proliferation,
apoptosis, metabolism, and invasion^[5]. In HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer, the
*PI3K-AKT-mTOR* pathway is activated in approximately 30-50% of
tumors. Activation results from mutations in *AKT1* or changes in
upstream signaling pathways including loss of function in phosphatase and tensin
homolog (*PTEN*) and mutations in the catalytic subunit alpha of
phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (*PIK3CA*)^[6],[7].

Capivasertib is an oral kinase inhibitor of AKT1, AKT2, and AKT3, which
inhibits phosphorylation of substrates downstream of AKT. Preclinical data
demonstrated differential activity of capivasertib by presence of mutations in the
*AKT* pathway. *In vitro*, capivasertib reduced
growth of breast cancer cell lines including those with relevant
*PIK3CA*, *AKT1,* or *PTEN*
alterations^[8]. *In
vivo,* studies with mouse models including estrogen receptor-positive
breast cancer models with alterations in *PIK3CA*,
*AKT1*, and *PTEN,* demonstrated increased drug
activity in the presence of the biomarker^[9].

On November 16, 2023, FDA granted traditional approval to capivasertib in
combination with fulvestrant for the treatment of adult patients with HR-positive,
HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer with one or more
*PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN-*alterations, as detected by an FDA-approved
test, following progression on at least one endocrine-based regimen in the
metastatic setting or recurrence on or within 12 months of completing adjuvant
therapy^[10] . Capivasertib
is the first tyrosine kinase inhibitor of AKT approved in oncology. Results from the
CAPItello-291 trial formed the basis for approval; this article summarizes
FDA’s rationale for limiting the indication to the biomarker-positive
population based on a favorable benefit-risk assessment in this population only.

#### PATIENTS AND METHODS

##### Clinical Trial Design

CAPItello-291 (NCT04305496) is an ongoing, randomized, placebo-controlled,
double-blind, multicenter study in adult patients with HR-positive,
HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer ([Figure 2]). The clinical study protocol and participant
informed consent documents were submitted for review to the institutional review
board (IRB) or independent ethics committee (IEC). Written informed consent was
obtained before a patient was enrolled. Eligible patients were required to have
disease progression on an aromatase inhibitor (AI)-based regimen in the
metastatic setting, or recurrence on or within 12 months of completing
(neo)adjuvant treatment with an AI and could have up to two lines of prior
endocrine therapy and one line of prior chemotherapy for metastatic disease.
Patients were randomized (1:1) to receive capivasertib plus fulvestrant or
placebo plus fulvestrant. Randomization was stratified by the presence of liver
metastases (yes vs no), prior treatment with a CDK4/6 inhibitor (yes vs no), and
geographical region. FoundationOne^®CDx next-generation
sequencing was performed on the majority of formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded
(FFPE) tumor specimens to identify eligible *PIK3CA/AKT1*
activating mutations or *PTEN* loss-of-function mutations.

The dosage for capivasertib was 400 mg orally twice daily for 4 days
followed by 3 days off. This dose regimen achieved exposure levels in a similar
range to that required to achieve activity in ER-positive breast cancer
nonclinical xenograft models. Clinical data established that a higher
intermittent dose could achieve equivalent efficacy compared with a lower dose
given continuously improved tolerability for some toxicities such as diarrhea
and rash^[11],[12]. Capivasertib dose-finding and dosage
optimization were not conducted for combination treatment regimens or in the
indicated patient population^[10].

##### Statistical Analysis

The dual primary endpoints were PFS assessed by the investigator
according to RECIST v1.1 in two groups: all patients randomized (overall
population) and the *PI3KCA/AKT1/PTEN*-altered population
(biomarker-positive population). The secondary endpoint of overall survival (OS)
was also tested in the overall population and the biomarker-positive population.
The PFS and OS endpoints were analyzed using the stratified log-rank test using
a truncated Hochberg procedure to control the overall type I error rate at an
alpha level of 5% (two-sided).

The assessment of efficacy was conducted using a pre-specified number of
events. The PFS in the overall population was tested first at 3.5%, and the PFS
in the biomarker-positive population was tested at 5%, after recycling the 3.5%
alpha. The statistical analysis plan was amended during the study to add a small
alpha spend to the assessment of OS at the time of the primary PFS analysis and
to remove an interim analysis of PFS. A sensitivity analysis was conducted using
PFS as assessed for all patients by blinded independent review committee (BICR).
The interim analysis of OS performed at the time of final PFS occurred at 40% of
required events for final OS. Other secondary endpoints included objective
response rate (ORR) and duration of response (DoR).

#### RESULTS

##### Efficacy

A total of 708 patients were randomized to receive capivasertib plus
fulvestrant (355 patients) or placebo plus fulvestrant (353 patients). A CONSORT
diagram detailing the breakdown of patients who were randomly assigned by arm
and alteration status is shown in [Figure 1].
The biomarker-positive population consisted of 289 patients, 155 in the
capivasertib arm and 134 in the placebo arm. The overall population also
included 313 biomarker-negative patients, 142 in the capivasertib arm and 171 in
the placebo arm, as well as 106 patients with unknown tumor alteration status. A
total of 106 (~15% of the overall population)patients’ tumors did
not have a result from biomarker testing and were not included in analyses of
either the biomarker-positive or biomarker-negative populations. [Table 1] summarizes baseline demographic and disease
characteristics of the patients in CAPItello-291.

As seen in [Table 2], [Figure 3], CAPItello-291 demonstrated a
statistically significant improvement in PFS by investigator assessment in the
overall population. Median PFS was 7.2 months (95% confidence interval [CI]:
5.5, 7.4) in the capivasertib arm compared to 3.6 months (95% CI: 2.8, 3.7) in
the placebo arm (Hazard Ratio [HR] 0.60 [95% CI: 0.51, 0.71]; p-value
<0.0001). CAPItello-291 also demonstrated a statistically significant
improvement in PFS per investigator assessment in the biomarker-positive
population (41% of the overall population), with a median PFS of 7.3 months (95%
CI: 5.5, 9.0) in the capivasertib arm compared to 3.1 months (95% CI: 2.0, 3.7)
in the placebo arm (HR 0.50 [95% CI: 0.38, 0.65]; p-value< 0.0001). In an
exploratory subgroup analysis of the biomarker-negative population (44% of the
overall population), the median PFS by investigator assessment was 5.3 months
(95% CI: 3.6, 7.3) in the capivasertib arm versus 3.7 months (95% CI: 3.5, 5.1)
in the placebo arm (HR 0.79 [95% CI: 0.61, 1.02]). Sensitivity analyses of PFS
by BICR were generally consistent with the analysis by investigator assessment
([Table 2]).

The first interim OS analysis performed at the time of final PFS
analysis was immature with 40% of required events; however, no potential OS
detriment was demonstrated in any population.ORR and DoR for the overall,
biomarker-positive, and biomarker-negative populations are shown in [Table 2].

##### Safety

###### Nonclinical Toxicology

In repeat-dose toxicology studies, toxicities were seen in
endocrine, cardiovascular, and hematopoietic systems and in male
reproductive organs, liver, and kidneys. Most capivasertib-induced
toxicities were reversible after a 4-week recovery period except toxicities
in the male reproductive system. In an embryo-fetal development study,
administration of capivasertib to pregnant rats resulted in embryo-fetal
mortality, fetal visceral variations, and reduced fetal weights. Based on
findings in animals and mechanism of action, capivasertib could cause fetal
harm when administered to a pregnant woman, and embryo-fetal toxicity was
included in the warnings and precautions section of the labeling^[13].

###### Clinical Safety

The safety of capivasertib was evaluated in the 705 patients who
received at least one dose of treatment in CAPItello-291, and this overall
safety population was used to characterize key safety signals. FDA
separately assessed safety in the biomarker-positive patients (n=288) to
characterize safety in patients included in the indicated population ([Table 3]). In both the overall
population and the biomarker-positive population, patients treated with
capivasertib plus fulvestrant experienced more toxicity than those treated
with placebo plus fulvestrant. The incidences of all-grade
treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), Grade ≥3 TEAEs, serious
adverse events (SAEs), and TEAEs leading to any dose modification were each
higher in the capivasertib arm than in the placebo arm.

Important safety signals for capivasertib plus fulvestrant include
hyperglycemia, diarrhea, and cutaneous adverse reactions. In the overall
safety population, hyperglycemia occurred in 18% of patients treated with
capivasertib. Grade 3 or 4 hyperglycemia occurred in 2.8% including the
life-threatening sequelae of diabetic ketoacidosis and diabetic metabolic
decompensation. Of patients with hyperglycemia, 45% required treatment,
including 15% requiring insulin, and most remained on antihyperglycemic
therapy at last follow-up.

Diarrhea was the most common TEAE, experienced by 72% of patients
treated with capivasertib, resulting in clinically important sequelae such
as dehydration and renal injury. Patient-generated data from
Patient-Reported Outcome Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events
(PRO-CTCAE) assessments demonstrated that most patients experienced loose or
watery stools with capivasertib, many “almost constantly” or
“frequently,” supporting the safety findings above. For
example, at the start of cycle 2, only 22% of patients on capivasertib and
55% of patients on placebo reported no diarrhea by PRO-CTCAE assessment.

Cutaneous adverse reactions occurred in 58% of patients treated with
capivasertib including Grade 3 or 4 events in 17% of patients. Cutaneous
adverse reactions include erythema multiforme and drug reaction with
eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS). Cutaneous adverse reactions were
the most common TEAE leading to dose modification of capivasertib, including
permanent discontinuation in 7% of patients. Of patients with cutaneous
adverse reactions, 44% required treatment, including 19% requiring systemic
steroids. Hyperglycemia and susceptibility to infections are relevant
potential risks of systemic steroids.

##### Companion Diagnostic

All patients enrolled in CAPItello-291 were tested for *AKT1,
PIK3CA,* and *PTEN* alterations.
FoundationOne^®CDx (F1CDx) next-generation sequencing (NGS)
assay was performed on the majority of FFPE tumor specimens to identify eligible
*PIK3CA/AKT1* activating mutations or *PTEN*
loss-of-function mutations. F1CDx is a qualitative NGS-based in vitro diagnostic
test. It was originally approved as a companion diagnostic in 2017 for the
detection of genetic alterations in patients who may benefit from FDA-approved
therapies for several tumor types including non-small cell lung cancer,
melanoma, breast cancer, colorectal cancer, and ovarian cancer.

Analytical and clinical performance of the assay supported the approval
of the F1CDx assay as a companion diagnostic device for identifying
*AKT1, PIK3CA,* and *PTEN*-alterations in
patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast
cancer. Clinical validity and performance were established in CAPItello-291.
This companion diagnostic was contemporaneously approved to identify patients
with breast cancer for treatment with capivasertib with fulvestrant^[14] .

##### External Evidence

During the evaluation of capivasertib, external evidence from the
FAKTION study was also considered. The FAKTION study was a Phase Ib/II,
double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized, study of capivasertib plus
fulvestrant versus fulvestrant alone in post-menopausal women with advanced
breast cancer previously treated with an aromatase inhibitor^[15]. Initially patients were assessed for
biomarker status by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for common
*PIK3CA-*alterations and immunohistochemistry (IHC) for loss
of PTEN. In a subsequent analysis, remaining patient tumor status was assessed
for *PI3KCA/AKT/PTEN*-alteration by NGS testing^[13]. In the analysis performed
after the NGS based testing, the median PFS of the overall population was 10.3
months (95%CI: 5.0, 13.4) in the capivasertib arm and 3.7 months (95% CI: 2.6,
3.8) in the placebo arm. The median PFS of 12.8 months (95%CI: 6.6, 18.8) in the
capivasertib arm and 4.6 months (95%CI: 2.8, 7.9) in the +in the
biomarker-positive population (HR=0.46; 95%CI: 0.28, 0.75). In contrast, there
was no meaningful benefit in the biomarker-negative population, with a median
PFS of 7.7 months (95%CI: 3.1, 13.2) in the capivasertib arm and 4.9 months
(95%CI: 3.2, 10.5) in the placebo arm (HR=0.72; 95%CI: 0.41, 1.27).

#### DISCUSSION

Capivasertib received traditional approval in combination with fulvestrant
for the treatment of adult patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative, locally
advanced or metastatic breast cancer with one or more
*PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN*-alterations as detected by an FDA approved
test. The FoundationOne^®CDx was approved as a companion diagnostic
device to identify patients with *PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN*-alterations for
treatment with capivasertib. The basis for the favorable benefit-risk assessment for
patients with *PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN*-alterations was demonstration of a
clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvement in PFS which was
robust to subgroup and sensitivity analysis and supported by an interim OS analysis
that did not indicate a potential detriment in the CAPItello-291 study. A
Post-marketing Commitment was issued with this approval to request the final OS
analysis from CAPItello-291. Although CAPItello-291 demonstrated a statistically
significant improvement in PFS in the overall population, capivasertib is a targeted
agent and must demonstrate a favorable benefit-risk profile in the overall
population and the biomarker-negative population in order to be indicated for the
overall population. When a trial of a targeted agent is not specifically designed or
powered to evaluate the biomarker-negative population, the benefit-risk assessment
will be based not only upon the efficacy in the biomarker negative-population
compared to the biomarker-positive population, but also biologic rationale,
prevalence and precision of the biomarker being investigated, safety profile, and
consistency of any external data.

In CAPItello-291, there was strong evidence of differential efficacy by
biomarker status. The improvement in median PFS in the biomarker-positive population
was 4.3 months by investigator assessment and 4 months by BICR assessment. In
contrast, in the exploratory analysis of the biomarker-negative population,
improvement in median PFS was 6 weeks by investigator assessment and approximately 5
days by BICR. In both CAPItello-291 and FAKTION, benefit of capivasertib in the
biomarker negative population was not clinically meaningful in the setting of a
poorly performing control arm and higher HRs of 0.79 and 0.72 respectively.
Similarly, the interim OS analysis demonstrated that the OS curve for the
biomarker-positive group demonstrated a trend toward benefit, with separation
throughout the assessment period. In the biomarker-negative population the hazard
ratio for capivasertib over placebo was close to one and the K-M survival curves
crossed multiple times making the interpretation uncertain ([Figure 3]). ORR and DoR results also showed substantively
higher activity with capivasertib in the biomarker-positive population.

The differential efficacy observed between the biomarker-negative and
biomarker-positive population is consistent with the biologic rationale that
capivasertib is a pan-*AKT* inhibitor that targets all three isoforms
of AKT and that the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway is often mutated in breast cancer leading
to anti-apoptosis, angiogenesis, and metastasis. Targeting AKT in those with tumor
mutations of this pathway may reduce tumor growth and the chance of metastasis.

External evidence from a different study in a similar population with the
same therapy may also support that the activity of the therapy is primarily observed
in a biomarker-positive population. Although the FAKTION study differed from
CAPItello-291, as no patients enrolled had received a prior CDK4/6 inhibitor and a
different biomarker testing method was used, the PFS analysis also suggested that
capivasertib appeared to have greater activity in the biomarker-positive population
than in the biomarker-negative population. The observation of differential efficacy
favoring the biomarker-positive population is less likely to be due to chance when
it is observed in two independently conducted trials (FAKTION and CAPItello-291) and
is consistent with the drug’s mechanism of action.

The precision of a biomarker assay to adequately identify patients who are
either biomarker-positive or biomarker-negative is important for the safe and
effective use of the treatment. A therapy associated with a precise biomarker such
as a gene alteration identified by an NGS test is more likely to identify a discrete
biomarker-positive patient population and may support an approval in this
population. Conversely, a therapy that is associated with an expression-based
biomarker scored on a continuum with no prespecified cutoff will make the definition
of biomarker populations more challenging. In CAPItello-291, the
FoundationOne^®CDx was used for tumor testing in the majority
of patients. Given the precision of this biomarker and its validation in the study
used to assess efficacy, a discrete biomarker-positive population could be
identified.

Approximately 40-50% of patients with HR-positive advanced or metastatic
breast cancer have one or more *PIK3CA* mutations,
*AKT* activating mutations, or *PTEN* inactivating
alterations (5). When a substantial portion of the patient population is
biomarker-negative, safety and tolerability risks become a special priority since
many patients could be exposed to the risks of a treatment without adequate evidence
of its efficacy. Exposing the nearly 50% of patients whose HR-positive breast tumors
do not harbor *AKT*-pathway mutations^[4] to the added toxicity burden of this
treatment would require specific and compelling evidence of its efficacy in this
population.

Finally, CAPItello-291 is a placebo-controlled trial with an add-on design,
and therefore, the improvement in PFS must be large enough to outweigh the added
toxicity of capivasertib to be considered clinically meaningful. The performance of
the placebo arm was modest with respect to PFS, and the median PFS improvement in
the overall population and the biomarker-negative population was considered marginal
and did not justify the added toxicity of capivasertib given the symptomatic and
serious adverse events. Capivasertib dose-finding and dosage optimization were not
conducted for combination treatment regimens or in the indicated patient population;
however, clinical data establishes that the intermittent dosing schedule may achieve
equivalent efficacy and improved tolerability when compared with a lower dose given
continuously.

Capivasertib’s approval represents progress for patients with
advanced and metastatic breast cancer. It is the first drug in its class approved in
oncology and offers selected patients a targeted treatment regimen. As we move
toward more precision in oncology therapeutics, the efficacy and safety analyses of
therapies should also evolve and become multi-dimensional. The CAPItello-291 trial
highlights that where a biomarker is incorporated in a clinical trial, regardless of
whether results provide evidence of superiority in the overall population, strong
evidence must be presented that the biomarker negative population benefit is also
robust. Scientific rationale, treatment landscape, and safety profiles should all be
considered in the context of efficacy data and statistical significance.

**Table 4: — FDA Benefit-risk assessment of Capivasertib for the Treatment of HR-positive/HER2-negative Advanced or Metastatic Breast Cancer**

| Dimension | Evidence and Uncertainties | Conclusions and Reasons |
|---|---|---|
| Analysis of Condition | • Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women, with 297,790 new cases estimated and 43,170 deaths estimated from breast cancer in the U.S. in 2023. Though rare, breast cancer does occur in male patients.• About 70% of breast cancers are hormone receptor-positive and eventually develop resistance to first-line endocrine based therapy.• Advanced or metastatic HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer remains incurable. | Advanced or metastatic HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer is a serious and life-threatening condition. |
| Current treatment options | • Treatment for advanced or metastatic breast cancer is palliative in intent with the goals to prolong survival, delay progression, and reduce symptoms from cancer.• Treatment options in the second line and later for patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative (IHC 0, 1+, 2+/ISH−) breast cancer include endocrine monotherapy with fulvestrant, an AI, or tamoxifen; everolimus in combination with exemestane, a CDK 4/6 inhibitor plus endocrine therapy; alpelisib in combination with fulvestrant for patients with PIK3CA-mutation; chemotherapy (capecitabine, eribulin, ixabepilone, paclitaxel protein-bound, gemcitabine) and/or an antibody-drug conjugate fam-trastuzumab-deruxtecan, recently approved for patients with HER2-low (IHC1+ or IHC2+/ISH−) who have received prior chemotherapy in the metastatic setting or developed disease recurrence during or within 6 months of completing adjuvant therapy. | There are limited options for patients with HR-positive advanced or metastatic breast cancer after progression on endocrine therapy and first CDK4/6 inhibitor. All treatment options are palliative and there is an unmet medical need to improve outcomes in these patients. |
| Benefits | • CAPItello291 enrolled 708 patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer who had disease progression on an aromatase inhibitor-based treatment and who could have received up to two prior lines of endocrine therapy and 1 prior line of chemotherapy for advanced or metastatic disease.• There were 289 patients (41%) in the biomarker positive population.In the overall population, median PFS was 7.2 months in the capivasertib arm and 3.6 months in the placebo arm (HR 0.60, 95% CI: 0.51, 0.71; P<0.001).• In the biomarker positive population, the median PFS was 7.3 months in the capivasertib arm compared to 3.1 months in the placebo arm (HR 0.050, 95% CI: 0.38, 0.65, P<0.0001). The OS HR was 0.69 (95% CI: 0.45, 1.05). The observed ORR was 29% (95% CI: 21%, 37%) in the capivasertib arm compared to 10% (95%CI: 5%, 16%).• In the biomarker negative population, the median PFS was 5.3 months in the capivasertib arm compared to 3.7 months in the placebo arm (HR 0.78, 95% CI: 0.61, 1.01). The OS HR was 0.90 (95% CI: 0.58, 1.39) and the KM curves crossed multiple times making this result uncertain. The observed ORR was 17% (95% CI: 11%, 25%) in the capivasertib arm and 14% (95% CI: 9%, 21%) in the placebo arm. | The PFS difference in the overall population is primarily driven by the biomarker positive population.For patients in the biomarker positive population, capivasertib demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in PFS, which is supported by no evidence of detriment in OS and an improvement in ORR when compared to placebo.For patients in the biomarker negative population, there is not a clinically meaningful improvement in PFS; and OS and ORR results add further uncertainty regarding clinical benefit in this population. |
| Risk and Risk Management | • The safety profile for capivasertib was similar in the safety population and the biomarker-positive safety population. Results are presented for the biomarker-positive safety population.• All-grade TEAEs and Grade ≥3 TEAEs were increased in patients in the capivasertib arm compared to patients in the placebo arm: 97% vs 84% and 42% vs 16%, respectively.• SAEs were higher in the capivasertib arm 18% compared to 11% in the placebo arm.• TEAEs leading to dose interruption, dose reduction and permanent discontinuation were higher in the capivasertib arm compared with the placebo arm: 39% vs 14%, 22% vs 0.8%, and 10% vs 2.3%, respectively.• The most common TEAEs (≥ 20%) including laboratory abnormalities were diarrhea, increased random glucose, cutaneous adverse reaction, decreased lymphocytes, decreased hemoglobin, fatigue, increased fasting glucose, nausea, decreased leukocytes, increased triglycerides, stomatitis, decreased neutrophils, and vomiting.• Capivasertib added significant toxicity to treatment with fulvestrant, particularly cutaneous adverse reactions, hyperglycemia, and diarrhea. | Safe use of capivasertib plus fulvestrant can be managed with labeling.Hyperglycemia, diarrhea, cutaneous adverse reactions, and embryo-fetal toxicity are included under Warnings and Precautions. There is no indication for a REMS.For patients with HR-Positive, HER2-negative, known non-altered advanced or metastatic breast cancer, the increased rates of symptomatic and serious TEAEs including the risks of hyperglycemia, diarrhea and cutaneous adverse reactions are not justified as the clinical benefit is marginal for this group. Overall, the benefit-risk assessment is not favorable for patients in the HR-positive, HER2-negative, biomarker-negative population. |
