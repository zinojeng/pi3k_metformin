# Alpelisib-Induced Diabetic Ketoacidosis and Insulin-Resistant Hyperglycemia

- **PMID**: 39896940
- **PMCID**: PMC11784619
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.aace.2024.10.002
- **Journal**: AACE Clinical Case Reports
- **Year**: 2025
- **Authors**: Loke Michael, Sehgal Vishal, Gupta Niraj

## Abstract

#### Background/Objective

Alpelisib is a phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase inhibitor used to treat certain hormone therapy resistant breast cancers that can cause hyperglycemia through inhibition of the insulin signaling cascade. Diabetic ketoacidosis with the initiation of alpelisib remains a rare complication. The objective of this report is to describe a patient with alpelisib-induced diabetic ketoacidosis and the difficulties of management.

#### Case Report

A 59-year-old woman was admitted to the hospital with a history of noninsulin dependent type 2 diabetes on metformin presented with diabetic ketoacidosis. One month prior to this hospitalization, the patient was started on alpelisib. On presentation, blood glucose level was 612 mg/dL and hemoglobin A1c level was 11.9% (107 mmol/mol), a 4.6% (27 mmol/mol) increase from 2 months prior. The patient was started on intravenous insulin and alpelisib was held resulting in rapid resolution of the patient’s hyperglycemia and ketoacidosis. However, with reinitiation of alpelisib the patient developed worsening hyperglycemia. Relative glycemic control was ultimately obtained with 3 oral agents and high doses of insulin.

#### Discussion

Direct inhibition of insulin signaling by alpelisib leads to insulin-resistant hyperglycemia. Most cases can be controlled with oral agents; however, insulin therapy is required in rare instances. Although more effective for glycemic control, insulin therapy has the potential to decrease the antitumor effects of alpelisib.

#### Conclusion

Diabetic ketoacidosis is a rare complication of alpelisib initiation, which is quickly resolved with cessation of the agent. For patients where cessation is not an option, insulin and insulin sensitizing agents can be used to achieve glycemic control at the potential detriment of tumor treatment.

#### Introduction

Alpelisib was approved in 2019 for hormone receptor positive (HR+), human epidermal growth receptor 2 negative (HER2–), phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) catalytic subunit alpha sequence variant advanced or metastatic breast cancer.[^1] Of breast cancer cases, as many as 68% to 78% are HR+,[^2] and PIK3CA sequence variation has been shown to be present in about 40% of HR+, HER2– breast cancers. This form of breast cancer tends to be resistant to hormone-based therapy and has a poor prognosis.[^3] PI3K inhibitors were shown to be effective for this subset of breast cancer in the SOLAR-1 trial. The trial showed a statistically significant difference in progression-free survival regardless of alpelisib dose.[^4]

Although therapy with alpelisib has been shown to be generally well tolerated, adverse effects include fatigue, hyperglycemia, anorexia, transaminitis, rash, nausea, diarrhea, mucositis, and dysgeusia.[^5] Hyperglycemia was found to be one of the most prevalent adverse effects at a rate of 59% to 62%.[^5]^,[^6] The level of hyperglycemia has been further graded based its severity. Grade 3 or 4 hyperglycemia was noted to occur in 28% of individuals with hyperglycemia occurring closely following initiation of the medication[^6]^,[^7] and resolving quickly with cessation of the agent.[^8] Individuals with diabetes or prediabetes, elevated body mass index, and advanced age have been shown to be at increased risk of hyperglycemia.[^7] Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) associated with alpelisib has been reported; however, it remains a rare occurrence with an incidence of 0.7%.[9], [10], [11]

The patient was initially diagnosed with HR+, HER2– infiltrating ductal carcinoma in 2016. She was treated with anastrozole, lumpectomy, and radiation therapy and achieved remission. In May 2023, the patient developed persistent arm pain. An X-ray was completed which showed evidence of pathologic fracture of the right humeral diaphysis with lucencies within the humerus concerning for metastatic disease. Further evaluation showed recurrence of the patient’s breast cancer. The patient underwent radiation therapy, and she was started on alpelisib in January 2024. Following initiation of alpelisib, the patient began to develop progressive fatigue, polydipsia, and polyuria. Pretreatment hemoglobin A1c level was 7.3% (56 mmol/mol) on metformin 1000 mg twice daily in December 2023, 2 months prior to this hospitalization, and at presentation to the hospital in February 2024, the patient’s hemoglobin A1c level was measured at 11.9% (107 mmol/mol).

Given her presentation in DKA, the patient was started on an insulin drip. Her alpelisib was also held, and the patient was able to transition off the insulin drip to subcutaneous insulin within 24 hours of admission. The hospital team discussed antineoplastic regimens with the patient’s oncologist. Alpelisib was determined to be the patient’s only therapeutic option given her metastatic disease and failure of hormone therapy in conjunction with surgical resection and radiation. Alpelisib was restarted on hospital day 2. Although maintained on the insulin drip, subcutaneous insulin and oral antidiabetic agents were initiated. #### Discussion

Alpelisib acts via inhibition of alpha-selective PI3K. PI3K is an essential enzyme that adds a phosphate group to phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate converting it to the active phosphatidylinositol (3, 4, 5)-triphosphate. Phosphatidylinositol (3, 4, 5)-triphosphate is then able to activate protein kinase B, also known as Akt[^3]. Akt along with extracellular signal-regulated kinase has also been shown to activate estrogen receptors in a hormone-independent pathway. This pathway is driven by growth factor-mediated receptor tyrosine kinase activity and has been linked to hormone therapy resistant forms of HR+ breast cancer making inhibition of this pathway an important target for therapy in this patient population.[^2] Akt is also essential for regulation of glucose, lipid metabolism, protein synthesis, and cell proliferation and survival.[^9]

Inhibition of PI3K leads to hyperglycemia by disrupting the signaling pathway of insulin. Insulin receptors are tyrosine kinase receptors that activate PI3K in the presence of insulin. This results in phosphatidylinositol phosphate conversion and Akt activation as noted previously. Akt then acts to maintain glucose homeostasis by inhibiting Forkhead box protein O1 transcription factor to regulate hepatic gluconeogenesis, activating glycogen synthase kinase 3 to induce glycogen synthesis in the muscle and liver, and inducing translocation of glucose transporter 4 receptors to facilitate insulin dependent uptake of glucose into adipose, muscle, and liver cells.[^3]^,[^12] Inhibition of PI3K and reduced activation of Akt leads to gluconeogenesis through uninhibited activation of Forkhead box protein O1, decreased glycogen synthesis through lack of glycogen synthase kinase 3 activation, and decreased glucose absorption with reduced presence of glucose transporter 4 at the cell surface. This ultimately shifts glucose homeostasis toward hyperglycemia.

For patients started on alpelisib, several recommendations have been proposed, lifestyle modifications including limiting carbohydrate intake to 30% to 40% of overall caloric intake; prioritizing a sugar-free, fiber-rich diet; and 150 minutes per week of moderate intensity exercise per week.[^3] Further investigation is currently underway regarding the use of very low carb or ketogenic diets in these patients. Baseline A1c screening and periodic monitoring should be completed. Those who have been deemed high risk warrant early endocrinology referral to optimize glycemic control prior to therapy and manage hyperglycemia during therapy.[^3]

For hyperglycemia related to alpelisib, antiglycemic therapy is used. Metformin has been widely accepted in the use of alpelisib-induced hyperglycemia; however, trials looking at efficacy are still pending. Sodium-glucose transporter protein 2 inhibitors (SGLT2-i) and pioglitazone have also suggested as beneficial due to properties that increase insulin sensitivity. The SOLAR-1 trial showed that oral antihyperglycemics are effective at improving glucose homeostasis, although 28% of individuals required 3 or more agents.[^7] Patients who are uncontrolled with oral antiglycemic agents or experience DKA can use insulin therapy; however, it should be avoided if possible as hyperinsulinemia may blunt the antitumor activity of alpelisib.[^13] If medical therapy is required glycemic control should target a fasting glucose level <160 mg/dL, random plasma glucose level <200 mg/dL, and hemoglobin A1c level ≤8% (64 mmol/mol).[^14]^,[^15] Discontinuation or dose reduction of alpelisib are the final options if glycemic targets cannot be achieved, with as many as 96% of individuals returning to their baseline fasting glucose levels with cessation.[^8] In cases of dose reduction or discontinuation, medical therapy should be reduced or discontinued, respectively.[^14]

In the case presented, the patient had an increase of A1c level from 7.3% (56 mmol/mol) to 11.9% (107 mmol/mol) in a 2-month period with the only notable change being the addition of alpelisib 1 month prior to the repeat A1c. Her initial presentation in DKA is notably abnormal, given the low incidence. The patient had quick resolution of DKA and hyperglycemia with cessation of the alpelisib and insulin administration. Based on the degree of hyperglycemia at presentation, cessation or dose adjustment of apelisib would be recommended.[^14] Unfortunately, alpelisib was the patient’s only therapeutic option and after it was restarted the patient developed hyperglycemia resistant to increasing doses of insulin. Further control was able to be obtained by adding insulin sensitizing agents, including metformin, pioglitazone, and sitagliptin. Despite increased oral therapy, the patient required continued insulin therapy. It remains unclear whether additional glycemic control and insulin reduction could be achieved by adding other oral antiglycemic agents. The use of an SGLT2-i has been shown to be beneficial in similar case studies.[^11] Despite the efficacy of glucagon-like peptide-1 agonist agents in type 2 diabetics not receiving antineoplastic therapy, their use in alpelisib-induced hyperglycemia remains unknown. The significant insulin requirement also raises the question of whether the antitumor effects of alpelisib are being negated in the attempts to control an adverse effect.[^15]

In conclusion, alpelisib is a PI3K inhibitor approved for therapy of HR+, human epidermal growth receptor–advanced or resistant breast cancer. The medication has been shown to be effective and is generally well tolerated with reversible adverse effects. One of the major adverse effects is hyperglycemia which has been shown to be easily reversible with the discontinuation of the agent; however, for patients who have failed first-line therapy and must remain on PI3K antitumor therapy, antiglycemic options are limited. For this patient population, further investigation into insulin sensitizing agents, such as metformin, SGLT2-i, and glucagon-like peptide-1 agonists needs to be completed. The use of insulin for sustained glycemic control outside of DKA remains controversial. The risk of sustained hyperglycemia versus cancer morbidity and mortality remains difficult to assess and should be addressed on a case-by-case basis.
